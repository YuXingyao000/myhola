# An ordinary occupied small office

Create a believable small office for everyday computer, administrative, and
document work, photographed from several positions. It should feel occupied and
maintained, with work temporarily paused;
people are not required. This is an ordinary office, not a workshop or showroom.

This task uses `quality_only`. The supplied Shed images establish expectations
for asset detail, materials, construction, spatial credibility, and lighting.
They do not prescribe decoration, layout, furniture, or lighting design. Build
independently from individual assets and authored architecture.
Do not locate or import the reference's underlying project, or substitute another
complete furnished interior.

There is no target CAD object or future product placement at this stage. Do not
reserve a display zone, choose a product pose, or arrange everything for one hero
camera.

## Activity and layout

Choose a coherent office activity and explain it in the scene plan. Decide room
dimensions, workstation count, furniture arrangement, and supporting facilities
yourself. Balance desk work, document handling, storage, and shared functions
according to that activity; these may share compact areas.

Use metric scale. Relate desk heights, chair
positions, monitor placement, reach, storage access, and walking routes to the
same working environment. Leave usable desk space, legroom, and room to move
chairs or open doors and drawers. Avoid uniformly spaced furniture, obstructed
circulation, or a few props stranded in an oversized room.

Give the room a complete enclosure: floor, walls, ceiling, doors, and appropriate
openings. Include visible construction such as frames, skirting, joints, window
reveals, switches, sockets, and service routes where the design calls for them.
Windows and open doors need intentional exterior or lighting treatment.

## Furniture, equipment, and signs of use

Choose convincing desks, office chairs, computers or laptops, input devices, and
storage appropriate to the activity. Prominent items need recognizable
construction: believable chair supports and upholstery, desk edges and legs,
monitor stands and housings, and functional storage fronts. Equipment should
have plausible power and data connections. Route cables toward reachable
outlets, docks, or power strips, with sensible slack and supports; avoid both
wireless-looking disconnected equipment and decorative tangles.

Use the supplied asset tool to inspect and acquire individual assets. Prefer free
resources whose actual licenses permit the intended use and packaging.
Record source URLs, authors, licenses, and dependencies through the prescribed
provenance workflow. Free download alone is not a license. Inspect previews and
imports for scale, geometry, UVs, maps, and completeness. Preserve attached
components and useful materials. Authenticated providers require configured access.

Simple architecture and secondary objects may be authored procedurally. Defining
furniture and computer equipment must withstand the inspection views; generic
boxes with decorative buttons are insufficient. If a critical asset cannot be
obtained or represented convincingly, name the precise gap and report incomplete
work rather than silently changing the brief.

Suggest recent activity through selected papers, notebooks, stationery, folders,
personal belongings, or refreshments appropriate to the work. Choose the details;
this is not a required shopping list. Use uneven purposeful groups near their
users, varied orientations, and some clear surfaces. Storage should serve office
use. Avoid random clutter everywhere or identical desk arrangements.

## Materials, maintenance, and light

Choose ordinary office finishes. Distinguish painted walls, flooring,
laminate or wood, fabric or mesh, plastic, coated metal, glass, and screens where
present. Inspect existing PBR maps before modifying them. Keep grain, weave,
seams, normal strength, and roughness variation at plausible scales. Large walls,
floors, and desktops must hold up in both wide and close views.

Show restrained use and maintenance: localized chair tracks, polished contact
areas, small edge scuffs, or subtle wiped surfaces where appropriate. Cleaned
areas and slight wear should follow activity. Do not use pervasive grime,
industrial residue, uniform brown noise, or exaggerated damage to imply realism.

Light the actual office through plausible windows, ceiling fixtures, and any
task lamps. Visible sources should agree with illumination direction, size, and
color. Preserve contact shadows, screen and window reflections, material
separation, and useful shadow detail. Avoid camera-facing studio fill, clipped
windows, excessive screen glow, or darkness and blur that conceal defects.
Keep geometry, objects, and lighting consistent across views.

## Six-view evidence and delivery

Choose accessible camera positions and sensible fields of view. The historical
`workbench_*` IDs refer here to desk work and the principal office activity.

| View ID | What the image should show |
| --- | --- |
| `room_overview` | Office enclosure, workstation arrangement, and circulation. |
| `room_reverse` | A substantially different direction revealing access, storage, and furniture relationships. |
| `workbench_context` | The principal desk activity with seating, equipment, and nearby working materials. |
| `workbench_detail` | Closer evidence of furniture and equipment construction, connections, and purposeful use. |
| `surface_detail` | A prominent desktop, floor, wall, or upholstered surface exposing material scale and condition. |
| `alternate_view` | Another meaningful relationship between foreground, working area, and background. |

Follow the supplied workflow to build, render, open, and improve actual images.
Deliver the reusable template, six views, provenance, and remaining defects.
Preserve previews and review records. An agent's `ready` means ready for human
review, not human acceptance.
