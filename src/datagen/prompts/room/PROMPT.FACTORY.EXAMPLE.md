# A working small machining factory

Create a believable small machine shop used for everyday metal-part production
and maintenance. This is a complete factory interior that can be photographed
from several positions. Use the supplied reference images as examples of material,
construction, spatial, and lighting quality. They depict another kind of room;
do not copy its layout or load its underlying project. This task uses
`quality_only`: construct the space independently from individual assets and
authored building elements, with no supplied factory project.

There is no target CAD component at this stage. Do not reserve an empty display
area, select a future product pose, or organize the factory around one camera.

## Activity and scale

Depict an ordinary working day in a small, established metalworking business.
Choose a coherent machining activity that the available credible equipment can
support, and describe it in the scene plan. The dominant equipment and its working
area must read as a machine shop, rather than a domestic repair bench or storage
shed with an industrial color palette. No people are required.

Select the actual equipment types, count, room dimensions, and layout yourself.
They should support the chosen work and remain credible together. Consider stock
or incoming parts, the machining operation, tool access, measurement or inspection,
and temporary storage of completed work. These functions may share compact areas;
they do not each need a separate room or a prescribed prop count.

Use metric scale. Relate machine dimensions, operating positions, tool storage,
work surfaces, doors, windows, and circulation to a human-sized working space.
Keep access to the equipment and storage plausible. Avoid oversized props,
crowded unusable aisles, uniform spacing, or a few objects lost in a large void.

## Architecture, equipment, and layout

Build a coherent enclosure with walls, floor, ceiling or roof structure, doors,
and any openings required by the design. Include construction details that are
visible at the chosen scales, such as frames, supports, transitions, and service
routing where appropriate. Every inspection direction should remain inside a
credible environment; visible windows or open doors need an intentional exterior
or lighting treatment.

Acquire convincing individual machine, furniture, and tool assets using the
supplied asset tool. Inspect previews and actual imports for geometry, scale,
material maps, and useful completeness. Preserve dependencies such as controls,
guards, cables, and attached fittings. Prefer assets suitable for an openly
reproducible research template, retaining their actual sources and licenses.
Do not assume a credentialed source is available unless it is configured.

Simple construction and secondary items can be procedural: shelves, modest bins,
stock offcuts, or distant fasteners need not each come from an external library.
Prominent machines must have credible recognizable construction and detail.
Do not fabricate a finished-looking machine from a generic box with arbitrary
buttons, or substitute a hand drill for the factory's defining equipment. If a
critical asset cannot be obtained at the required quality, record the precise
gap and report incomplete work instead of silently changing the scene brief.

Arrange related items around plausible tasks: tools near their working positions,
material near processing or storage, and a small amount of work in progress.
Use uneven groups and orientations without distributing clutter randomly. Keep
functional clear areas and quieter regions as part of the occupied space.

## Surfaces and condition

The workshop has been used for years and is maintained. Choose credible floor
and wall finishes for this particular space rather than applying one flat color
to each large surface. Inspect suitable PBR materials at their actual scale.
Show the distinct responses of painted machinery, bare metal, rubber, concrete,
masonry, wood, plastic, or other materials only where they belong.

First inspect the selected asset's existing maps. Add wear only where needed:
handled surfaces may polish, machine bases may show localized residue, and
working or traffic areas may have scratches, repaired patches, or cleaned marks.
Keep these linked to use and maintenance. Avoid uniformly rusty machines, a
universal brown noise layer, excessive bump, or repeated identical wear on
neighboring assets. Large floors, walls, and equipment surfaces must remain
credible in the wide views as well as closer inspection.

## Lighting and photography

Design illumination for the actual building and work. Choose plausible daylight,
general work lighting, and any local task lights; decide their locations and
balance from the room layout. Visible fixtures and openings should agree with
the direction, size, and color of the light they imply. Keep structural occlusion
and believable contact shadows. Metallic equipment should reflect the room and
neighboring objects with varied, plausible highlights.

Maintain useful differences between brighter working areas and quieter storage
without losing the evidence needed to judge geometry and surfaces. Avoid a
uniformly lit display studio, arbitrary camera-facing fill lights, clipped broad
highlights, excessive color casts, or deep darkness used to hide unfinished work.
Keep the scene intact across views, without camera-specific visibility tricks or
strong depth of field that hides weak assets.

## Six-view evidence and delivery

Choose accessible camera positions and sensible fields of view for these roles:

| View ID | What the image should show |
| --- | --- |
| `room_overview` | The factory enclosure, major equipment, and functional layout. |
| `room_reverse` | The same room from a substantially different direction, including access and storage relationships. |
| `workbench_context` | The principal machining area in context, including equipment, the operating position, and nearby tools or material. This need not be a workbench. |
| `workbench_detail` | A closer view of important equipment and its actual construction, material response, and surrounding task details. |
| `surface_detail` | A prominent working, floor, wall, or equipment surface at a scale that exposes texture and wear quality. |
| `alternate_view` | Another spatial relationship with meaningful foreground, activity area, and background depth. |

Use the prescribed workflow to build, render, open, and improve the actual
images. Deliver the complete reusable template, all six views, provenance, and
an honest account of remaining defects. Preserve earlier previews and review
records. The final human review will judge room identity and proportions, major
surfaces and equipment, lighting and reflections, and consistency across all
six images. An agent's `ready` decision means ready for that review, not that
the human has accepted the result.
