# An independent electronics repair studio

Create a believable electronics repair studio in regular use by a small
independent business. Build a complete interior, convincing from several positions. Use supplied
references, including Shed, only for construction, material, spatial, and
lighting quality.
This task uses `quality_only`: assemble an original room from individual assets
and authored building elements. Do not locate or import the reference project,
copy its layout, or substitute another furnished interior.

There is no target CAD component or product-placement task. Do not reserve an
empty display area or compose the room around a future object or one camera.

## Work and everyday life

Choose a coherent repair specialty, such as compact audio equipment, small test
instruments, or everyday consumer electronics, and explain it in the scene plan.
The room should communicate diagnosis, careful disassembly, repair, and testing
through the equipment and its relationships. Choose the room dimensions, furniture, layout, and equipment quantities yourself. No people are
required.

Connect incoming devices, active work, component storage, and completed repairs
in a plausible daily workflow. These can share space. A device under repair should have related parts, tools,
and documentation nearby; detached covers and retained screws should belong to it. Choose a
few purposeful signs of interrupted work rather than scattering electronic
components over every surface.

Make this an ordinary, maintained workplace. A marked repair sheet, labeled
parts tray, or jacket near the entrance can suggest an established routine. Preserve clear working areas, reachable controls, chair movement,
and usable circulation. Use metric scale throughout.

## Architecture and defining equipment

Build the entire room: floor, walls, ceiling, entrance, and appropriate openings. Include credible construction at visible
scales, with frames, junctions, sockets, supports, and service routing where
appropriate. Furnish the surroundings as well as the principal bench. Windows
and open doors need an intentional exterior or lighting treatment.

The defining equipment must make electronics repair legible. Select credible
diagnostic, inspection, and repair tools appropriate to the specialty. An
oscilloscope, bench supply, microscope, multimeter, or soldering station should
have recognizable construction when used, including meaningful controls,
connectors, supports, and accessories. Select what the chosen work needs.

Show practical relationships: probes can reach their work, cables connect to
plausible endpoints, and a soldering tool has a suitable holder and local fume
extraction. Use antistatic surfaces and storage where appropriate to exposed
boards. Keep liquids, loose metal debris, and packaging away from delicate
active work. Avoid impossible wiring, floating test leads, decorative circuit
boards, and identical generic boxes dressed with arbitrary buttons.

Use the supplied asset tool to acquire convincing individual equipment,
furniture, and material resources. Inspect previews and imports for scale, completeness, geometry, and maps.
Preserve attached parts and dependencies.
Prefer resources suitable for an openly reproducible research template, keeping
actual sources and licenses. Simple shelving, trays, or secondary construction
may be procedural. If defining equipment cannot be represented convincingly,
record the precise gap and report incomplete work.

## Materials and use

Choose finishes suited to a modest repair business. Painted or laminated
furniture, antistatic matting, molded plastic, coated metal, glass, cable rubber,
and paper should respond differently to light. Inspect existing asset maps
before adding wear, and keep texture scale credible across the room.

Concentrate use traces where the work explains them: slight mat discoloration,
handled knobs, fine scratches near a frequently used tool, or chair wear on the
floor. Include maintained surfaces and newer replacements.
Avoid universal grime, rusty industrial styling, repeated scratches, excessive
bump, or a brown noise layer covering everything.

## Lighting and photography

Combine plausible daylight with general room lighting and local task lights.
Let window placement and working positions determine their balance. Fixture
direction, size, and color should agree with the illumination they produce.
Provide usable light for precise bench work without flattening the rest of the
room. Preserve contact shadows, readable dark plastics, controlled screen
brightness, and varied reflections on instruments and glass.

Use accessible cameras and sensible fields of view. Keep the room intact across
views; avoid visibility tricks, heavy blur, or darkness concealing unfinished areas.
